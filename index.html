Peptide guide

The Wolverine Blend

BPC-157 and TB-500 together, for helping the body repair its tissues. It gets the name from Wolverine, because of how he heals.

What it is

Peptides are short chains of amino acids, the same building blocks as protein. Your body already makes thousands of them as messengers. These two send a repair message, and they work beautifully side by side.

BPC-157

A short piece of a protein found in our own stomach juices. BPC stands for Body Protection Compound. It is best known for supporting the gut lining, tendons and ligaments, and for helping blood flow reach tissue that is trying to heal.

TB-500

A lab-made version of the active part of thymosin beta-4, a protein found in almost every cell in the body. It helps repair cells move into an injured area, supports new small blood vessels, and helps calm inflammation.

BPC-157 works more on the spot that needs repair. TB-500 travels further through the body. Together they cover both, which is why they are so often taken as one blend.

What people use it for

  • Repairing the gut lining, including leaky gut
  • Healing after surgery
  • Tendon, ligament, muscle and joint injuries
  • Connective tissue that is slow to repair
  • Ongoing inflammation in the body
  • Supporting collagen

How to take it

  • It goes just under the skin with a small insulin syringe. Most people use the soft tummy area, about 5cm out from the belly button.
  • Wash your hands, and wipe the vial top and your skin with an alcohol swab first.
  • Pinch a little skin, slide the needle in, press the plunger slowly, and let go of the pinch.
  • Take it at the same time each day. Morning or night, whichever you will remember.
  • Move to a new spot each day, left side then right side, so the skin stays happy.
  • One syringe, one use. Never share.

Storing it

  • Before it is mixed, keep the powder somewhere cool and dark. The fridge is perfect.
  • Once it is mixed with water, it lives in the fridge. Never the freezer, and never in a hot car or a sunny spot.
  • Use a mixed vial within about 4 weeks.
  • Don't shake it. Swirl gently if you need to.

What you might notice

Most people feel very little apart from the healing. The things people most often mention:

  • Some redness, itch or a small bump where the needle went in
  • Feeling warm or flushed for a little while
  • Mild tiredness, light-headedness, a headache or a little nausea in the first few days

These usually settle quickly. If they don't, message Kelly and we'll adjust.

Swelling of the face, lips or throat, hives all over, or any trouble breathing: stop and call 000 straight away.

Talk to Kelly before you start if

  • You are pregnant, trying to fall pregnant, or breastfeeding
  • You have cancer now, or have had cancer, including skin cancers. Both peptides encourage new blood vessel growth, so this needs a proper conversation first.
  • You have a bleeding or clotting disorder, or take a blood thinner
  • You have surgery booked
  • You are under 18
  • You compete in drug-tested sport. Both are on the World Anti-Doping list.

The honest bit

Most of the research on these two has been done in the lab and in animals. Human studies are still small. Neither is an approved medicine in Australia. Many people, practitioners and athletes use them with great results, and Kelly is happy to walk you through what is known.
